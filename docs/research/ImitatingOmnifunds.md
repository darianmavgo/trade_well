Omnifunds deliberately avoids trading stocks that overlap an earnings report.
The basket of stocks is NAS100, Top Tech, and RUSS3000. Not etfs. 
Every week or day it runs in-house analytics to predict which stock is most likely to gain that week. They don't share what the algo is. 
Position sizing is capped at 50% of available cash.

I found it. OmniFunds isn't a fund. It's auto-trading software you license from Intelligent Fund Management, which is built on Nirvana Systems in Austin, TX. It places trades in your own brokerage account, held at Gar Wood Securities and cleared through Interactive Brokers. Your money is never pooled, and the company says plainly that it isn't a registered investment adviser or a fiduciary. (

Here's how it trades, based on the company's own materials:

Ranking: Every day it scores its stock list for which names are most likely to rise over the next week or month. It swaps out weaker holdings for higher-ranked ones whenever the ranking or its read on the market changes. It doesn't trade on a fixed calendar.
Concentration: Most funds hold only 3 to 4 stocks, some up to 5. The "50%" you mentioned is its cap on any single stock, so one name can be half of what it's trading. That's a concentrated bet.
Execution: Orders go in at the market close by default. Some funds move to cash, or even inverse ETFs, when it reads the market as bearish.
Earnings filter: It stays out of a stock for a set number of days around its earnings report and can sell beforehand.
Exits: There's no built-in stop-loss or profit target beyond the ranking itself. You can add your own stop-loss, but anything it sells for that reason sits in cash until the next signal.
Fees: A one-time license that "starts around $10,000," plus $99 to $499 a month depending on how much it trades, plus commissions of about $0.005 per share. (

The cautions:

The returns are unverified. Their claims of over 65% to 78% in the past year are their own figures, with no audit behind them. Their charts mix in simulated backtest results, and every trade before March 2024 is simulated. On Reddit and PissedConsumer, users are mixed to skeptical, saying live results lag the charts and refunds are hard to get. I didn't find any SEC or FINRA action against OmniFunds itself. Gar Wood, the broker, was fined twice by FINRA, in 2013 and 2022.
Taxes: It switches holdings every week or so, so in a taxable account nearly all of your gains are taxed as short-term income. Wash-sale rules will also come up a lot, since it often sells a stock and buys it back within 30 days.
The best way to judge it is your own account, not their marketing.
